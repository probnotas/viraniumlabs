Working notes from the lab. Each entry corresponds to one rung of the ladder we are climbing, and each rung is a single question that can be answered no.

EPI Labs is a research lab betting against data. We study how little experience a robot needs to learn a predictive model of its own body — and how fast it can repair that model when the body wears, slips, or breaks.

Rung 3 · Technical report · September 2026

Surprise Has a Signature

Before a self-model adapts, it has to decide whether its own body changed or the world briefly acted on it. We freeze a learned forward model, balance Pendulum-v1 with CEM, and at four seconds either lengthen the pendulum permanently or apply a 1 N·m torque for a quarter of a second. The brief push produces the larger spike — 0.164 against 0.092 — so magnitude does not separate the two. Persistence does: three seconds on, the body change is still at 5.71× its pre-onset error against 1.06× for the push. One episode per condition at one setting, so this is a direction result rather than a classifier, and it reports no false-positive rate.

Rung 2 · Technical report · August 2026

A Wrong Model Is Worse Than None

Damage detection and recovery in a learned self-model, and why model-based control is the more brittle option. A pendulum's mass is raised 50% mid-experiment. The model notices immediately — one-step prediction error rises 9.1× under random probing — and fine-tuning recovers 43% of the lost control from ten new transitions. The headline we intended did not survive its own control experiment: against a model-free policy pre-trained on the healthy body, model-based control degraded 139% where the reflex policy degraded 26%. We argue the defensible value of a self-model here is not planning but fault detection.

Rung 1 · Technical report · August 2026

The Planner Hides the Model

Why control cost is the wrong instrument for measuring sample efficiency in model-based control. Models trained on 50 samples controlled a pendulum as well as models trained on 5000, and the null result turned out to be an artifact of the measurement rather than a fact about the model: held-out prediction error fell 8× across the same sweep while a random-shooting planner converted none of it into better actions. A control curve measured without a separate model-quality probe is uninterpretable.
